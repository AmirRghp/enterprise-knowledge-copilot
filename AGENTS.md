# Project instructions

Read [docs/AGENTS.md](docs/AGENTS.md) and the relevant core documents before substantial work. That file centralizes this project's agent instructions; keep this root file as a discovery pointer.

Per the current rule in `docs/AGENTS.md`, each task must use skills: read the local vendored `SKILL.md` linked in `plans.md`/`docs/AGENTS.md` for the 2-4 relevant skills before doing the work, and report which skills were used in that part's summary. Project documents override generic skill advice.

Part 7 adds the Ollama embedding adapter with the Nomic prefixes, bounded retry, and vector validation under apps/api; it is approved and published on origin/main. Embedding sends `truncate: false` so over-long input errors instead of being silently trimmed, validates exactly 768 finite values, re-reads the model digest before every embedding and fails closed when it differs from the required `OLLAMA_EMBED_DIGEST`, and bounds each attempt with a real wall-clock deadline. Part 8 then adds the durable ingestion worker under apps/api/app/ingestion with the manager upload routes in apps/api/app/api/manager.py: a `.md`/`.txt` upload is validated before any write, staged as a pending job with a server-generated document id and revision, claimed with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED under a 120-second lease renewed every 30 seconds, and every staging write and activation is fenced on a per-claim token and on the lease expiry, so an expired lease is refused even when nobody reclaimed the job; the expiry is compared against the database's live clock rather than now(), because now() is the transaction start time and goes stale while a transaction waits on a lock; a statement's qualification is also evaluated before its wait, so the check is repeated after the lock is held, and activation repeats it once more after its last lock (advisory, then job row, then document row) and immediately before any mutation, so no wait that outlives the lease can slip through. Activation is one transaction that compare-and-swaps the expected current revision and atomically reserves the chunk quota under the publisher lock, so a failed job leaves the previously published revision searchable and two concurrent publishers cannot both take the last room. The upload route parses no body until Origin and the manager role have been checked, and an ASGI guard caps the whole multipart body while streaming. Part 8 is approved and published on origin/main.

Part 9 added the operator corpus import (`uv run --locked python -m app.cli.import_corpus`), which validates all 20 fixed corpus files before any write, preserves their document ids and takes each role from an operator allowlist; it is idempotent and rejects an existing id whose text or policy differs. It also completed the manager page at `/manager/documents` with the list, upload, job status and safe Persian error messages, polling every 5 seconds. Part 9 is approved and published on origin/main.

Part 10 (document replacement and logical delete) is approved and published on origin/main. Migration `0002` adds two nullable columns, `documents.staged_access_role` and `documents.staged_title`, and its constraint names are wrapped in `op.f()` in both directions so the `ck_` naming convention is applied exactly once; without that the upgrade doubled the prefix and the downgrade could never drop the constraint. A document's effective `access_role` and `title` never hold a not-yet-activated value: narrowing access is applied immediately when the replacement is staged and is deliberately *not* rolled back if the replacement fails, while widening and the title are applied only at activation, in the same transaction that publishes the text. `app/ingestion/sections.py` keeps section identifiers stable across a replacement — preserved sections are matched by title and a new section takes an identifier above the highest one that document has ever used, never a recycled gap, because the section id is the citation handle. `store.stage_replace` refuses a second non-terminal job for one document with 409, which covers the create job too, since a document that has published nothing has no revision to replace. `store.soft_delete_document` is logical and idempotent: it keeps `current_revision` and the published rows, fails every open job and clears its lease so an in-flight worker stops at its next fenced write, clears the staging columns, and removes revisions that were never published — taking locks in publisher advisory → document row → job rows order, matching `activate_revision`'s lock order. Activation additionally refuses an inactive document. The web proxy allows `PUT`/`DELETE` on one document with an allowlisted id shape, requires the web Origin on every write, and answers `204` without inventing a body; `UploadGuard` now covers `PUT` as well as `POST`, so an oversized replacement is refused before it is buffered.

Part 11 (authorized hybrid retrieval) is implemented and validated but **approved and published on origin/main**. The `apps/api/app/retrieval/` package builds the allowlist as a **SQL predicate**, not a post-filter: one statement carries `status = 'active'`, `current_revision IS NOT NULL`, an `EXISTS` for a `ready` job on that revision, and the permitted `access_role`, and both search paths are built from it. Filtering after ranking would leave the database ranking rows the actor may not read, which makes the hit count depend on forbidden documents and leaves safety resting on nobody forgetting the filter. BM25 matches on **token overlap, never on the score's sign**: `rank_bm25` floors a negative IDF at a fraction of the corpus's average IDF, and on a small corpus where a term appears in every document that average is negative too, so a perfectly matching chunk scores negative and a `score > 0` test returns nothing at all. Two further properties the review pinned down: the fused candidates are re-read under the allowlist **immediately before returning**, because two reads in one snapshot do not stop an access narrowing that lands between them, and `store.assert_index_consistent` compares the stored provider/model/digest/dimension against the live provider **before anything is ranked** - scoped to the eligible domain rather than the whole table, so one actor's availability never depends on rows they may not read. The question is normalized once and that one string goes to both paths, and a provider failure exits with a fixed message and a non-zero code rather than a traceback, because the adapter's message can quote back the question. Part 12 (optional rerank) is implemented and validated but **approved and published on origin/main**. Rerank sits between RRF and the final cut in `app/retrieval/rerank.py`: at most `rerank_top_k` fused candidates, then the reranker, then at most `final_top_k` results. `RERANK_ENABLED` defaults to false and a disabled rerank performs **zero** provider work - no client, no adapter, no request. This is the first time document text leaves the process, so the allowlist gate from Part 11 is re-run through the same `read_ranked_chunks` before every send **including retries**: the body is not prepared in front of the retry loop, because a prepared body is the second attempt replaying the first attempt's text after an authorization revocation that landed during the backoff. `arun_with_retry` calls a per-attempt factory, that factory re-runs the gate and rebuilds the body from the freshly read rows, `expected` provenance is checked on those same rows before anything is sent, and a refresh that comes back empty fails closed instead of sending a shorter list. The user reviewed this part twice. The first review found four defects, including that version built the request body once in front of the retry loop; all four were fixed, and each fix was proved by deliberately reintroducing the defect. A second review on 2026-10-07 found one more P2: after a successful attempt `_map_results` still used the caller's original `candidates` and `requested`, so a retry that rebuilt a different list scored the wrong chunk and a retry that sent fewer documents was checked against the wrong expected count. The adapter now returns `(payload, sent, effective_top_n)` and maps against the successful attempt's list. Part 13 follows the same two rules: every generation attempt re-runs the egress gate through `read_ranked_chunks` and rebuilds the user turn from that attempt's own evidence, so a revoked or replaced passage cannot reach the provider on a retry or a repair, and `ProviderUsage` counts every attempt rather than only the successful one, because an unusable answer was still billed. An empty refresh is its own `GenerationEvidenceError` rather than a response error: nothing was sent, so there is no schema anyone ignored, it stops immediately because a repair would ask the model again with evidence the caller may no longer send, and the report maps it to "no authorized evidence" rather than to a schema failure that would send an operator looking for a broken endpoint. Usage is finalized in a `finally` rather than at each exit: the list of ways the attempt loop can leave keeps growing, so finalizing by hand per `raise` meant a billed response followed by an empty refresh reported no cost at all, and a permanent `403` carrying usage lost it the same way. Keeping the figure is not enough on its own: every failure exit of both commands now prints a sanitized usage line on stderr, because an accounting nobody can see leaves the process with the operator, and the preflight is the run whose cost matters most when the free endpoint allows fifty requests a day. At the service level the consequence was silent data loss rather than a failure - the wrong indices attached scores to the wrong chunks, one of them a revoked document, and the egress gate then dropped it, so the ranking quietly lost a result. Only `index` maps a score back, and it is checked with `type(value) is int` rather than `isinstance`, because JSON `true` decodes to `bool` and `bool` subclasses `int` - an `isinstance` check would read `true` as index 1 and attach a real score to the wrong chunk. A wrong result count, a repeated or out-of-range index, a non-finite score and a missing usage figure all fail closed; a missing usage figure is recorded as unknown rather than zero, because a zero cost is a claim about money that the provider never made. Enabled without a provider is a wiring error, not a degraded mode, and an enabled failure is visible: there is no fallback. Part 14 adds `apps/api/app/answer/`: `quotes.py`, `validate.py`, `compose.py` and `service.py`, plus the operator command `app/cli/answer_report.py`. Three things in it exist because of measurement, not preference. First, the quote rule: **87 of 109 chunks in this corpus contain a ZWNJ** - 459 occurrences across 33,262 characters - so a hundred-character quote has roughly a 75% chance of containing one and a bare `quote in source` test rejects about three out of four valid quotes. The matcher therefore tolerates a length-preserving character map and contiguous token runs, but **returns the passage's own span**, so the served citation is always text the document contains; the token boundary is derived from `normalize_for_search` itself rather than from a second character list that could drift. Contiguity is required - two real fragments spliced together quote nothing - and the floor is two tokens rather than a character count, because Persian words are short enough that any character floor excluding one word would also exclude real policy phrases. Second, **the final gate runs before validation**: the cited passages are re-read under the allowlist in a fresh session, and a missing one means a narrowing, deletion or replacement landed in the window between the provider call and the delivery, which refuses the whole answer. Gating first proves the quote against text that was authorized at the last possible moment and makes a separate identity assertion unnecessary; the re-read also settles that a replacement cannot return new text under old ranking, because `read_ranked_chunks` requires `revision = current_revision`. The revocation check is scoped to handles actually *offered*, so a handle the model invented is reported as `UNKNOWN_EVIDENCE_ID` rather than as a closed document. Third, `MAX_CLAIMS` lives in the answer layer and not in `GENERATION_SCHEMA`, which has no `maxItems` on `claims`: an unsupported parameter is silently ignored by the provider, and with `require_parameters: true` that turns into a rejected request. The `GenerationProvider` protocol was also brought in line with the adapter - it lacked `refresh`, and Part 14 is the first caller that must pass the gate through the protocol, so without that either `answer` used `Any` or cast past the checker and dropped the egress gate silently. **Measured on the live free endpoint: zero served answers from seven attempts.** Two `quote_not_found`, one abstention with no claims, one provider error. Structural diagnosis showed the matcher is not at fault - E3's quote validated with 17 of 17 tokens contiguous, while E2's had 17 to 18 of 18 to 19 tokens present but **out of order**, longest aligned prefix zero. The free model rarely quotes contiguously on this corpus. That is a fact about the model, not a defect in the validation, and no claim is made about another model or prompt; loosening the matcher to improve that number is precisely what this part exists to prevent. Quote validation proves *grounding* - the claim rests on text the authorized passage really contains - and not that the sentence follows from it, which `evaluation-guide.md` scores by human review. Retrieval never writes, has no fallback path, and exposes no text: `app/cli/retrieval_report.py` prints identifiers, ranks, the fused score and the role it applied, never chunk text, section source, document title or the question itself.
